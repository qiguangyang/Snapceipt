Search "best receipt scanner app" and you'll get a hundred listicles, most of them written for an American audience. The trouble is that a great receipt app in the US isn't automatically a great one here. Australia has its own quirks — GST at 10%, the quarterly BAS, the ATO's record-keeping rules — and an app that ignores them just leaves you doing the local maths yourself. This guide lays out what actually matters when choosing a receipt scanner app in Australia, then compares the main options honestly so you can pick the right one for your situation.

## What to look for in an Australian receipt app

Before you compare brands, get clear on the job. A receipt scanner should save you the boring parts of bookkeeping, not add new ones. Here's what's worth holding out for if you're an Australian sole trader or small business:

- **It reads GST automatically.** In Australia, GST is 10% and works out to one-eleventh of a tax-inclusive price. An app that pulls the GST line off each receipt saves you that calculation on every purchase — and if you're registered for GST, those amounts are your input tax credits.
- **It produces BAS-ready records.** If your GST turnover is $75,000 or more you must register for GST and lodge a [Business Activity Statement](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/business-activity-statements-bas/goods-and-services-tax-gst), usually quarterly. The right app lets you read your GST totals straight off, instead of re-tallying slips the night before it's due.
- **It keeps ATO-compliant digital records.** The ATO accepts a clear digital copy as a valid record, and expects you to keep records for five years. A good app stores a legible image, not just a number you typed in. For the detail on what counts, see [keeping receipts the ATO accepts](/guides/do-you-need-paper-receipts-ato).
- **It works in AUD by default.** Sounds obvious, but plenty of global apps assume US dollars and date formats until you wrestle the settings into shape.
- **It's private and cloud-synced.** Your receipts should outlive any single phone, and back up somewhere you trust.
- **It's fairly priced** for one person, not just a 50-seat finance team.

## The main options in 2026

No single app is "the best" for everyone — it depends on whether you need GST and BAS, how many receipts you scan, and how much you want to spend. Here's an honest read on the common choices.

**Snapceipt** is built in Australia for sole traders and households. Snap a receipt and it reads the merchant, total, GST and category, syncs to the cloud, and rolls your figures into a GST/BAS export. It also handles quotes and invoices. The trade-off: it's focused on Australian receipt and expense tracking rather than being full double-entry accounting software, so a larger business with payroll and inventory may eventually want more.

**Expensify** is a polished, widely used expense tool. Its SmartScan reads the merchant, date and amount and supports 150-plus currencies, and there's a free tier with a monthly cap on scans. It's genuinely global, which is its strength and its limit — it isn't built around Australian GST and BAS, so AU tax handling is on you.

**QuickBooks Online** has a proper Australian edition that tracks GST automatically, prepares a BAS summary, and can e-lodge straight to the ATO, defaulting to Simpler BAS. It's strong if you want full accounting, though that also makes it heavier and pricier than a focused receipt app — arguably more than a one-person operation needs just to log receipts.

**Zoho Expense** offers autoscan that reads receipts (including line items), has a dedicated Australian edition and a GST-aware interface, and a free tier for small teams. It's a capable, expense-report-oriented tool that leans toward businesses with staff submitting claims.

**Wave** is popular for being free, but it's centred on the US and Canada. Australian support is limited, and reports indicate receipt scanning has been pulled for non-US users — so it's worth checking current availability before relying on it here.

## How to choose for your situation

Match the tool to how you actually work.

If you're a **sole trader or freelancer**, you want the least friction: capture a receipt in seconds, get the GST read for you, and export BAS-ready totals each quarter without a bookkeeping degree. A focused, Australian-built receipt app like Snapceipt fits this neatly, especially if you also send the odd quote or invoice. The pillar guide on [tracking business expenses in Australia](/guides/track-business-expenses-australia) walks through the wider routine.

If you run a **small business with staff or stock**, you may outgrow a pure receipt scanner and want full accounting — QuickBooks or a similar ledger — with a receipt tool feeding into it. Decide whether you're buying a books system or a capture tool; they're not the same purchase.

If it's for a **household** tracking the family budget, rebates or shared expenses, you don't need GST or BAS at all — prioritise quick capture, easy search and reliable cloud backup over tax features.
